Research profile

Research and technical interests

I study how material architecture, contacts, and boundary conditions influence mechanical response. My work combines computational mechanics, composite testing, and numerical programming.

CURRENT PHD WORK

Fiber network mechanics

Fiber-scale finite element models help me examine contact, compression, and the effect of lateral and periodic boundary conditions on network response. I work with beam models, nonlinear contact, and simulation diagnostics to connect local interactions with bulk behavior.

EXPERIMENTS AND MATERIALS

Composite characterization

My coauthored NASA technical report compares the quasistatic properties of continuous-fiber HexPly and discontinuous-fiber TuFF composites. The work draws on mechanical tests, stress–strain curves, and property extraction across multiple loading directions.

PRIOR RESEARCH

Impact and damage modeling

My master's thesis developed a solid-element model of soft body armor under ballistic impact. It compared backface signature and layer penetration with a test and examined sensitivity to mesh resolution and material parameters.

METHODS AND TOOLS

Scientific computing

I use Abaqus, LS-DYNA, Python, MATLAB, and C++ for model building, finite element analysis, data extraction, and numerical methods. My programming projects include matrix operations, structural solvers, plane elasticity, and computational fluid dynamics.
